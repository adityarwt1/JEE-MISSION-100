Question:

$$
\frac{\text{Electric flux}}{\text{Magnetic flux}}
=
[M^P L^Q T^R A^S]
$$

Find \(Q\) and \(R\).

---
Electric flux:

$$
\Phi_E=E\times \text{Area}
$$

Magnetic flux:

$$
\Phi_B=B\times \text{Area}
$$



$$
\boxed{
\left[\frac{\Phi_E}{\Phi_B}\right]
=
\left[\frac{E\cancel{A}}{B\cancel{A}}\right]
=
\left[\frac EB\right]
=
[M^P L^Q T^R A^S]
}
$$

---

Electric field:

$$
E=\frac{F}{q}
$$



$$
[E]=\frac{MLT^{-2}}{AT} ;  \Big[  [F]=MLT^{-2}, [q]=AT \Big]

$$

$$
\boxed{[E]=MLT^{-3}A^{-1}}
$$

---


Lorentz force:

$$
F=qvB
$$

So,

$$
[B]
=
\frac{MLT^{-2}}
{(AT)(LT^{-1})}
$$

$$
[B] = \frac{MLT^{-2}}{ALT^{1-1}}
$$
$$
[B] = \frac{MLT^{-2}}{ALT^{0}}
$$
$$
[B]=\frac{MLT^{-2}}{AL}
$$

$$
[B]
=
\frac{M\cancel{L}T^{-2}}
{A\cancel{L}}
$$

$$
\boxed{[B]=MT^{-2}A^{-1}}
$$

or

$$
\boxed{[B]=M^1L^0T^{-2}A^{-1}}
$$

---

$$
\boxed{
\left[\frac{\Phi_E}{\Phi_B}\right]
=
\left[\frac EB\right]
}
$$


$$
\left[\frac EB\right]
=
\frac{[MLT^{-3}A^{-1}]}
{[MT^{-2}A^{-1}]}
$$


$$
\left[\frac EB\right]=M^{1-1}L^1T^{-3-(-2)}A^{-1-(-1)}
$$

$$
\left[\frac EB\right] =M^0L^1T^{-1}A^0
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\left[\frac{\Phi_E}{\Phi_B}\right]
=
M^0L^1T^{-1}A^0
}
$$

Compare with:

$$
[M^P L^Q T^R A^S]
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{P=0}
$$

$$
\boxed{Q=1}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=-1}
$$

$$
\boxed{S=0}
$$

Question asks \(Q\) and \(R\):

$$
\boxed{(Q,R)=(1,-1)}
$$

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{Option D: }(1,-1)}
$$

**Ek important logical shortcut:**

$$
\frac{\Phi_E}{\Phi_B}
=
\frac{EA}{BA}
=
\frac EB
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\frac EB=\text{velocity}}
$$

therefore directly:

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Phi_E}{\Phi_B}\sim LT^{-1}}
$$

so \(Q=1,\ R=-1\).
